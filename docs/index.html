Language models · compression · forecasting

Damaged language models forget in a predictable order.

Prune it, quantize it or fill it with noise. What a language model loses first is decided before the damage, by three properties of what you ask it. So it can be forecast before a compressed model is deployed.

Finding 1

Seven damages. One order.

Noise, zeroing, neuron ablation and layer removal break a model in very different ways. Pruning and quantization choose what to remove on purpose and could have rewritten the order. They did not: damage sets how fast capabilities fall, not which fall first.

Damage strength 1.00×

At strength 1.00× the lines are measured: accuracy kept at the dose that halves each model's overall ability, mean of eight models, short-answer format. Other strengths follow the paper's rank-one model, in which a damage turns one dial and each capability fails at its own rate: kept = exp(−n·h). ρ, rank correlation of each damage with the mean order. Median agreement over all model pairs: 0.90 between random damages, 0.875 between compression and random damage.

Finding 2

Three properties of what you ask set the order.

They act separately and add up: a task effect plus a format effect explains a median of 89% of how fast each capability fails.

1Task

Less sure, fails first

Tasks the intact model holds with a smaller margin between the right answer and the best wrong one break first.

schematic
Smaller margin, earlier failure in every model (Spearman 0.58–0.73). Fragility falls with margin as a power law, exponent near 2.
2Fact

Rare facts go first

Within a task, facts whose answers are rare in pretraining text vanish before common ones. The long tail goes, the core stays.

Each dot is a capital, left to right by how often its answer appears in pretraining text; dots vanish in the order measured in Gemma-2-9B. Rarer answers fail first in all 8 models (median Spearman 0.42) and under all 7 damages.
3Format

Writing fails before choosing

The same question, answered by picking, by writing the answer, or inside a template. The more the model must produce exactly, the sooner it breaks.

fragility vs choose
Fragility is 1.5×, 2.5× and 8× that of choosing. Format moves a capability as far as the task does.
Finding 3 · why it matters

Know what compression will cost, before you deploy.

Measure a model's fragility profile once, or borrow it from other model families. After compressing, test four fixed tasks. The profile then predicts the accuracy every other capability keeps.

One example: Qwen2.5-7B after Wanda pruning to 63% sparsity, answer format; calibration on WinoGrande, OpenBookQA, PIQA and copying a word. Across all 23 model and method combinations, the profile had 44–46% less error than a uniform decline in every one (median error 0.110–0.118 vs 0.200–0.213), close to the difference between two runs of the same damage (0.117). All preregistered.

Before compressing

Know what goes first

A profile measured once under random damage predicts the order of losses under Wanda, SparseGPT and GPTQ, down to individual facts.

After compressing

Test 4 tasks, not all of them

Four calibration tasks give the accuracy every other capability keeps, each held out completely.

For a new model

Skip the profiling

A profile borrowed from other model families, excluding the model's own, predicts about as well as the model's own.

Beyond engineering

The same order as a damaged human brain.

In semantic dementia, rare knowledge and naming fail first. Lesioned networks of a few hundred units reproduced this decades ago. We find it in networks of billions of parameters, under random lesions and under compression.

🧠 Patients with semantic dementia

  • 1
    Rare, atypical knowledge is lost before familiar knowledge
  • 2
    Naming fails before recognition
  • 3
    Errors drift toward familiar items

🤖 Damaged language models

  • 1
    Facts with rare answers fail firstpositive in every model and task cell
  • 2
    Writing an answer fails before choosing it1.5× more fragile, even scoring content only
  • 3
    A wrong capital is usually the more frequent onein 62–100% of cases

The resemblance concerns what is lost and in what order, not shared mechanisms. It suggests that how robust a capability is depends on what a network has learned rather than on how it is damaged.
